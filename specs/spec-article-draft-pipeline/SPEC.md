# Article Draft Pipeline

## Why

A pain to solve plus an opportunity to capture: the owner wants a publishable technical-article draft in ~10 minutes of personal attention, from raw material already in git (dev logs, specs, READMEs, commit history, BMAD memlogs). The owner's initial workflow idea — human extracts key points, AI reconstructs narrative — spends scarce human minutes on the step AI does better (extraction) and delegates the step that most needs human control (claims and voice). This pipeline inverts that: AI harvests with source pointers, the human contributes judgment through a bounded interview and a verification pass. Distribution, not artifact quality, is the current bottleneck (ratified owner finding); this pipeline is the throughput fix.

## Capabilities

- **CAP-1** (rewritten 2026-08-02, #1220/#1223 — stated over `examine`, not over a fact sheet)
  - **intent:** Every claim the draft asserts is **grounded at the read that produced it**: given a concrete claim, the pipeline judges whether it holds and records where the evidence sits, as a resolvable pointer (file/line, commit, or URL). There is no pre-extraction pass and no standing fact sheet — grounding is per claim, at the point the claim exists.
  - **success:** Auditing any grounded claim leads to the exact source location; a claim with no resolvable pointer is classified `[VERIFY]`, derived, or narration under CAP-3, never asserted as sourced.
- **CAP-2** (amended 2026-07-11 per `docs/interview-architecture.md` D1–D3)
  - **intent:** The pipeline interviews the owner with at most 5 targeted questions covering only what sources cannot answer — surprise, significance, opinion, warnings. Every candidate question is triaged against the harvest output (fact sheet + NEEDS-OWNER list — recommendation generation is a view over harvest output and reads nothing else) into exactly one of three outcomes: **suppressed** (fully covered — never reaches the owner), **recommended** (a groundable, source-pointed candidate answer exists, including recorded owner judgments in sources — presented as the default choice with approve/modify/replace/skip dispositions; NEEDS-OWNER re-raises are always recommended, as confirm/deny of the claim), or **open** (genuinely owner-only knowledge — bullet free-text). Stage 2 writes an interview journal to the run workspace recording each question's outcome, survival rationale, grounding pointers, and disposition.
  - **success:** A run on a real project completes the interview in ≤5 questions and ≤5 minutes; a question fully covered by the fact sheet never reaches the owner and its suppression is journaled with the covering entries; every asked question's survival rationale (topic-absent / needs-owner-reraise / owner-judgment) is attributable from the journal without owner intervention; an approved recommended answer carries its source pointers into the draft's provenance.
- **CAP-3** (amended 2026-07-11 per `docs/harness-architecture.md` D1–D2; amended 2026-07-20, #439; amended 2026-07-20, #440/#434)
  - **intent:** The pipeline fills the chosen framework (from SPEC-article-frameworks) to produce a draft with schema-conformant frontmatter, classifying every sentence into one of three provenance classes recorded in a sidecar provenance map: a **sourced claim** carries a fact-sheet or interview pointer; a **derived claim** compresses, combines, or restates ≥2 named sourced claims and inherits their pointers — introducing new causality, significance, evaluation, comparison, intent, or scope makes it inferred; **narration** asserts nothing checkable (falsifiability test) and needs no pointer. Inferred claims — asserting more than sources, interview, or derivation support — carry inline `[VERIFY]` markers as before. **Owner opinion as attributed prose (amended 2026-07-20, #439):** owner opinion the interview elicits — thesis, arc, stakes, beliefs and reversals — may enter the draft as **owner-attributed prose spans** classified `sourced`, carrying a **paragraph-granularity interview (question-id) pointer** rather than a per-sentence pointer; this is the prose-shaped channel for the owner's story, distinct from flattening it into atomic sourced claims. The falsifiability contract is unchanged — such prose asserts nothing source-checkable and must not — so it is compatible with the narration rule while remaining explicitly attributed; `verify-provenance` accepts a paragraph-granularity question-id pointer as a valid `sourced` attribution for the span it covers. **Argument-plan sub-step (amended 2026-07-20, #440/#434):** fill **opens with an argument-plan sub-step** — before populating sections it composes an explicit **argument plan** (thesis, arc, per-section content intents) from the fact sheet (including narrative kinds, #438) and interview, and fills **from that plan**, not the framework skeleton directly. A framework governs each section's **content obligations, not its literal heading structure**; a multi-lesson article is a single arc (shared context, varied lesson sections, one synthesis), never the skeleton repeated verbatim per unit. The plan is a **run-workspace intermediate**, owner-visible during the run — not a new pipeline stage, and provenance is unchanged. At completion the plan-record `plans/<slug>.md` (SPEC-article-plan, **unchanged**) **projects** thesis/arc/section-intents from the finalized plan (declared single-authority — the completion record is the projection-of-record; never two independent plans). **Composition over an owner-selected Strand set (amended 2026-07-30, #945 — clauses in `amendments.md`):** a **cover counted in placements**, checked **after** composition (every selected Strand placed or its omission disclosed by name), and a **single-axis default rule** (a declared default resolves a one-axis choice, visible and overridable in the plan; multi-outcome-with-no-policy-basis fires the gate).
  - **success:** For a produced draft, every claim-classed sentence traces to a source pointer, an interview answer, inherited pointers, or a `[VERIFY]` marker; an independent `verify-provenance` check finds no narration-classed sentence asserting a checkable proposition and no derived claim exceeding its inputs; frontmatter passes build validation (AC-4).
- **CAP-4** (amended 2026-07-18, #361/#362 — the variant stage is not part of the draft flow)
  - **intent:** Platform-ready variants — a dev.to copy (full text, `canonical_url` placeholder) and/or a Zenn repo-sync copy (Zenn frontmatter), per the article's language and canonical policy (AP-6) — are produced by a **separate post-review invocation** that consumes the **persisted, reviewed canonical** at `output.drafts`, never a run-workspace intermediate. The draft flow itself ends by durably writing its two declared products (canonical + plan); it offers no platform decision.
  - **success:** Each variant is publishable on its platform without manual reformatting beyond filling the canonical URL; a completed draft run leaves `drafts/{slug}.md` and `plans/{slug}.md` persisted with no platform variant emitted; grepping the draft-flow stages for a platform decision point finds none.
- **CAP-5** (added 2026-07-10)
  - **intent:** Before any generation or review work, the pipeline validates the resolved configuration (`user-config.yaml`, `writing-sources.yaml`): unresolved placeholder values (example-file defaults such as the pointer-block site name), malformed URLs (e.g. double slashes in `canonical_url` composition), and missing required keys are reported as configuration errors up front — publishing configuration is a precondition, never an article-quality finding discovered at the end of the pipeline.
  - **success:** Running the pipeline against a config still carrying an example placeholder or a malformed URL halts at stage 0 with a per-key report naming the file and fix; a clean config produces no configuration findings anywhere later in the run.
- **CAP-6** (added 2026-07-10; amended 2026-07-15, #226)
  - **intent:** Every run — including standalone harvest — ends with a completion summary partitioned into exactly three labeled buckets (**informational notes**, **publish blockers** — e.g. unresolved `[VERIFY]` markers, unrendered figures, a quality-gate failure surviving its revision cycles (CAP-7), **optional cleanup**) followed by an explicit next step. No run ends by only reporting results. **Interaction contract (#226):** every human decision point — the next step included — is presented **in-conversation** (a selection UI where available: e.g. after a standalone harvest, "continue into draft-article / stop here", drafted from the fact-sheet and NEEDS-OWNER counts); local artifact paths (fact sheet, run workspace, logs) may be **displayed informationally** — a copy-pasteable path remains required output — but opening or navigating a local artifact is never a prerequisite for continuing the workflow. A **resumed or partially-completed run** (durability constraint above) reports its last completed stage and the resume path under informational notes, so partial progress is never a silent loss. The **reading-time estimate** (words ÷ ~200 wpm EN, characters ÷ ~500 cpm JA) is an informational-bucket item **only for runs that produce or review an article body**; standalone harvest has no article body to measure and omits it.
  - **success:** A run with a known blocker lists it under publish blockers and nowhere else; every run's summary — harvest included — contains the three bucket headings and a concrete next step presented as an in-conversation choice, with no step that requires the owner to open a machine-state artifact to proceed (paths still printed for reference); an article-producing or article-review run additionally shows a reading-time estimate, while a standalone harvest run shows none.
- **CAP-7** (added 2026-07-11 per `docs/harness-architecture.md` D3–D5; amended 2026-07-20, #440/#434)
  - **intent:** A mandatory article-quality gate sits between stage 3 and stage 4 — a stage-progression precondition like the `[VERIFY]` exit criterion, never an advisory finding. The draft must pass a fixed four-dimension rubric (narrative arc, paragraph flow, explanation calibration, readability mechanics; the rubric is a versioned plugin asset), judged by zero-token mechanical checks (dimensions 3 and 4 — dimension 3 amended 2026-07-17, #305: a deterministic vocabulary scan against the rubric's written introduction contract, emitting the complete violation set in one verdict, with audience-known terms excluded via the per-run owner-ratified allowlist) plus one single-pass cheap-tier rubric judge (dimensions 1–2 as the gate; it may emit dimension-3 findings as advisories only, never a gate verdict), **run in a subagent that never saw the drafting turn** (the same isolation NFR13 sets for `verify-provenance`, so the drafting context never grades its own rubric pass), emitting pass/fail per dimension with failing locations, in findings format, no rewritten text. On failure, stage 3 revises against the named failing dimensions and re-runs **both** the quality gate and `verify-provenance` — at most two revision cycles; a failure surviving them surfaces as a publish blocker (CAP-6) naming the failing dimensions and locations, never silently retried or waived. **The second cycle's re-run is a bounded delta re-check, not a fresh full-artifact re-judge (added 2026-07-18, #349): the mechanical dimensions (3–4) re-run in full — they are deterministic and cannot drift — but the cheap-tier dim1–2 judge is scoped to VERIFY that cycle-1's failing locations were addressed; it may not introduce a new interpretive dim1–2 finding, so a genuinely new problem is actionable on the second cycle only when a mechanical dimension flags it. Isolation (NFR13) is preserved: the second-cycle judge still never saw the drafting turn — it receives cycle-1's failing LOCATIONS as its scope, never prior verdicts as "context to be better informed by". And splitting a sentence to satisfy dim4 (readability mechanics) is not scored as a dim1–2 regression — the rubric resolves that conflict, so the two cycles converge instead of oscillating.** **The convergence half of that rule extends to the PROVENANCE judge (amended 2026-08-02, #1287): a provenance verdict is keyed by (position, hash of that position's segmented sentence text) and CARRIED for the whole run across every judge round — the pre-gate fill respawn rounds included — so a position whose keyed text is unchanged is never re-graded, pass **or** fail, and a verdict disagreeing with a carried one on identical text is a disclosed defect rather than absorbed cost. The clause quoted immediately above ("so the two cycles converge rather than oscillate") was written for the rubric judge alone; the provenance judge is the same shape and the #738 delta amendment extended the delta's SCOPE to it without its convergence half. This bounds WASTE, never COST: it caps no number of judge rounds, and rounds that grade text which actually changed still run.** **Plan-conformance + strengthened narrative arc (amended 2026-07-20, #440/#434):** the **narrative-arc dimension is strengthened** — it must **fail** a draft that reads as a stitched fact sheet or repeats a section skeleton verbatim per unit (drafts it passes today), and a **plan-conformance** check joins the **same** gate: a draft whose sections do not advance the argument plan's thesis (CAP-3), or that fills a slot with a single under-evidenced sentence, fails the Stage 3→4 gate **before** review — review is a second net, never the first. This **strengthens the existing gate**; no separate gate is added, and the `>70%-sourced-with-no-tissue` detector remains the skeleton alarm.
  - **success:** A fact-sheet-stitched draft (wall-to-wall sourced claims with no derived/narration tissue) fails the gate and never reaches stage 4 unrevised; a gate failure after two revision cycles appears in the completion summary's publish-blocker bucket with dimensions and locations; a passing draft enters stage 4 having consumed zero minutes of the owner's attention budget on quality retries.
- **CAP-8** (added 2026-07-20, #432 — per the ratified depth-over-count policy, owner decision record 2026-07-20)
  - **intent:** Article scope, depth, and density are **owner editorial intent set per artifact, never a tool default**. A run may carry an optional **depth/scope directive** captured at stage 0 — or, absent one there, offered as a stage-2 interview item — either a level (`deep-dive | standard | note`) or an explicit one-line scope statement. **Stage-3 fill consumes it**: the directive governs how much each slot elaborates and how many story elements the draft carries — it is **not** a word count or reading-time target. **Reading-time-driven splitting is demoted to a declinable suggestion:** the reading-time estimate stays an informational-bucket item (CAP-6) and drives no split, and any framework's count/length split guidance (e.g. a lessons framework's ">N → split") is a **suggestion the owner may decline**, surfaced as an in-conversation choice under the proposal contract, never an automatic cut. With **no directive**, the run behaves exactly as before this capability (the framework's own defaults), so the directive is an enhancer, never a new required gate. **The offer is a mandated-tier item, guaranteed outside the ≤5 cap (amended 2026-07-22, #542):** absent a `--depth` directive, the depth/reading-time offer is presented **exactly once on every fresh or re-opened/narrowed run** — it is not a NEEDS-OWNER candidate and is never displaced by the ≤5 cap — and if it is not presented, the completion summary **discloses its absence and the applied default** (a run that both lacks a depth directive in run-state and shows no depth item in the presented-payload capture (#234) is a disclosure/validation failure, not a silent default). "Enhancer, never a required gate" means fill still proceeds on the framework default when the owner declines — it never means the offer may be silently skipped. **Reading-time as the owner's expression unit (added 2026-07-21, #506):** the stage-0 / stage-2 depth question **may** present suggested reading-time bands derived from the selected elements (e.g. `~3 min note / ~7 min standard / ~15 min deep-dive`) plus a custom value; the owner's pick is recorded **as** the depth directive (mapped to a level or one-line scope statement), **not** as a reading-time target. Fill still elaborates by depth semantics, the estimate stays informational (CAP-6), and nothing ever auto-splits or auto-trims to hit the number — a large miss between the chosen band and the estimate surfaces as an informational FYI, owner decides. Reading-time is the unit in which the owner may *express* the depth choice, never a mechanical target the pipeline optimizes toward.
  - **success:** A run given `depth: deep-dive` produces one deeper article instead of splitting into several; a framework's split hint surfaces as an owner choice ("this would be ~N lessons — one deep-dive, or split?"), never an automatic split; a run with no directive is byte-for-byte the behavior before CAP-8; the reading-time estimate remains informational and drives no split.
- **CAP-9** (added 2026-07-20, #428/#430/#431 — story-element selection; distilled via `bmad-spec`, forks A2/B1/C1)
  - **intent:** A **story element** is a general **evidence cluster** — fact-sheet entries grouped by a **declared, deterministic membership rule**, a lesson being one case — each with a **stable id** that is its identity (the evidence-pointer set is derived payload under it; pointer drift on re-harvest never changes identity). Selection chooses which elements a lesson-based article covers, **upstream of the argument plan (CAP-3)**. The **selection rule is made explicit and disclosed** — the completion summary and interview journal state, per selected element, the rule that selected it (#428, **disclosure-only**: the base selection behavior is unchanged). Lesson-based selection **defaults to unconsumed elements**: a draft records its consumed elements **by id in the article plan** (`plans/<slug>.md`, SPEC-article-plan — **no new store**), and exclusion reads across `plans/*.md` (any index is a **mechanically regenerated view**, never a second ledger), with an explicit **owner override** (#430). An entry argument may **name an element**, pinning selection to it and scoping harvest to that element **without widening the declared-source boundary** (#431). Selection is **deterministic and auditable**, never a taste-ranked recommendation. **The entry argument is generalized to a free-form story proposal (amended 2026-07-22, #554 — ratified 2026-07-22, an owner decision record — 2026-07-22 (consumption and entry points) D7):** an entry may be the owner's **free-form message describing the story they want**, and the pipeline proposes **several concrete article STRUCTURES** for it — composed from that message × the fact sheet × policy — **not prose, and not a single take**. They are presented at **exactly one gate**, as machine-proposed **selectable options plus a free-form response** (approve / modify / replace / skip) per the human-gate presentation contract (`consulted: hub@<private-pin> topics/knowledge-architecture.md:14`); never raw-artifact homework, never more than one confirmation. **This generalizes the SHIPPED structure proposal (CAP-4, Story 18.26), it does not add a second proposer:** candidate structures, their element-grounded rationales, the marked default, the owner-facing presentation (pick one **or counter-propose free-form**), and the `structures` helper all already exist — what changes is the **input**, which now admits the owner's free-form message (the existing coverage brief, #505) alongside the selected elements. The **chosen structure continues to be recorded in the argument plan's `arc`** (CAP-3/CAP-4, unchanged) — **no new store and no second recording location**; in particular it is **not** written to `editorial_anchor`, which carries the claim/angle answer only. **Consumption/overlap exclusion gates what is SURFACED BY DEFAULT, never what the owner may pick here** (`threshold-gates-surfacing-not-permission`, `consulted: … LESSONS.md:41`): a free-form request naming an already-consumed element is **honoured**. The **named-element pin (#431) is the degenerate case of this entry point** — one element, named explicitly — and is **not a second mechanism beside it**; there is exactly one description of entry semantics. **Consumption is a selection-time DERIVED VIEW (amended 2026-07-22, #556 — ratified an owner decision record — 2026-07-22 (consumption and entry points) D1/D9/D3/D4):** an element is consumed iff **a live (non-graveyard) backlog/draft/published item cites it OR an ever-published item cites it** — recomputed at each selection from articles-repo state, **never a stored flag, never a consumed-ledger here, with no reversal operation**. The articles repo is **authoritative** (its frontmatter schema is the API); a selector that disagrees with repo state is the defect, and the mismatch check is the selection itself. The never-delete/`graveyard/` convention is a **correctness invariant** of this view, carried by an **existence lint**: a citation resolving to neither the live tree nor `graveyard/` is a repo defect, surfaced, never swallowed. **Open, not contracted:** the machine-readable Lesson-citation join key does not yet exist in the articles-repo schema; that one schema addition belongs to the **articles repo**, and the existence lint lands with it. Until then the `plans/*.md` exclusion above is this predicate's **current instantiation** — same shape, narrower join — and only its join widens.
  - **success:** A third article from the same repo states which elements it selected and why, skips elements the prior articles consumed (by id, from `plans/*.md`), and — when the owner names one — scopes the run to that element; membership and reasons reproduce from the declared rule.
- **CAP-10** (added 2026-07-21, #516; rewritten 2026-08-02, #1220/#1223; **corrected 2026-08-02, #1235** — the property is kept and the harvest-shaped machinery is dropped)
  - **intent:** Per-claim examination is **reproducible**: examining the same claim against the same pin resolves to the same grounding. One mechanism carries it — **deterministic enumeration**: candidate files enumerate in stable order (`resolve-writing-sources.py files` order, then in-file order), so what a claim was examined against does not vary between runs. **The model runs only inside per-claim judgment**; enumeration and ordering are deterministic tools. **The correction, recorded because the superseded text was written days earlier and asserted machinery that never existed:** the 2026-08-02 rewrite carried CAP-10's original three mechanisms forward — a per-source read budget and a blob-keyed extraction cache beside the ordering — on the reasoning that they "survive the stage reorder unchanged in substance". They do not survive, and they were never built for examine. Both were **harvest-shaped remedies for a harvest-shaped defect**: #514 was an attention collapse over a *whole-corpus pass*, the budget bounded *corpus growth*, and the cache was — in CAP-10's own original words — "an amortization of re-reads, never a correctness precondition". A per-claim read is query-scoped by construction and has neither failure mode, so building them to satisfy spec text would be machinery ahead of demand. **Reopen trigger, named rather than left to judgment:** an examine run measurably re-reading the same blob across claims within one sitting earns the cache; a per-claim read that grows with corpus size earns the budget. **What the stage reorder removed:** there is no whole-corpus extraction pass, no fact sheet, and — per the same day's #1224 amendment — no coverage manifest over a declared universe. Grounding is scored per claim, which is what makes it gradeable at all.
  - **success:** Examining one claim twice against an identical pin resolves to the same pointers in the same order; a claim that cannot be grounded is reported as an ungrounded **claim** with its reason, never as a verdict about the run; a source that was not reached is distinguishable from a source that was read and yielded nothing.

## Non-goals

- No publishing: the pipeline never calls dev.to/Zenn APIs or pushes to a Zenn-synced repo; the owner publishes.
- No review: quality passes are SPEC-article-review's contract.
- No **browsable candidate-story list** (article-creation entry point (a); added 2026-07-22, #554): entry point (b) — the free-form story proposal (CAP-9) — ships first as the **superset**, covering what a list would for any story the owner can name plus structural shaping a list cannot do, and needing no list infrastructure. A candidate list waits behind **its own demand trigger**; when it comes it is a **consumer-side regenerated view** over (fact sheet × policy × consumption status) — **no hub capability and no stored list**.
- No **stored consumption flag, consumed-ledger, or consumption-mirror** anywhere — tool or hub (added 2026-07-22, #556): consumption is recomputed at selection time from articles-repo state (CAP-9), so there is no state to reverse and no reversal operation; the hub stores unit existence, never per-consumer usage. No standing dashboard or candidate-list store follows from it either.
- No image *rendering* or hosting; evidence figures and rendered images are produced by the owner's tooling. *(Narrowed 2026-07-10 by SPEC-article-visuals: proposing visual **source** — Mermaid, figure specs, image-generation prompts — is that spec's contract and in scope for the pipeline; rendering remains out.)*
- No topic selection: the owner chooses what to write; the pipeline does not maintain an idea backlog.

## Success signal

The owner takes a real project (e.g. a QuantScenarioBench release) from "invoke pipeline" to a draft they would hand to review in ≤10 minutes of their own attention, and spot-checking the draft finds zero unmarked invented claims.

## Assumptions

- Drafting runs on the strongest model available to the owner (ratified owner decision: draft quality determines the number of downstream review cycles).

## Open Questions

- ~~Should the pipeline also create the site's `mode: external` article record for JA/Zenn pieces, or does that stay a manual follow-up?~~ **Resolved 2026-07-16 (owner decision record, site external record):** neither a manual follow-up nor a platform variant — a **post-publish proposal**. The record is not a variant: it fails the profile type on every field (no audience, no language register, nothing to lede-re-target, and it carries no claims — it is index-facing metadata: title, canonical URL, date), so modeling it as a "site profile" would be a degenerate profile instance and would breach the ratified site-independence boundary (the site is owner identity, never a pipeline emission target; its schema stays in user config). Structurally, its substrate completes only at the publish event — the load-bearing field is the *final* published URL plus the real publication date, which exist after the owner's manual publish act — so a variant-stage emission would be stale at birth. Contract: after the owner confirms the published URL (a CAP-6 in-conversation next step), the pipeline **proposes** a ready-to-paste record block conforming to the user-config site frontmatter schema (≤20 lines, body forbidden), under the owner-facing proposal contract; applying it to the site tree is the owner's (or a site-side command's) act — the pipeline never writes the site's tree.
